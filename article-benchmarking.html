Benchmarking Studies in Transfer Pricing

May 8, 2024 10 min read HexaTP Team

Benchmarking studies are the cornerstone of transfer pricing compliance. They provide the empirical evidence needed to support the arm's length nature of intercompany transactions. This comprehensive article explores everything you need to know about benchmarking studies.

What is a Benchmarking Study?

A benchmarking study is a detailed analysis that compares the terms and conditions of intercompany transactions with those of comparable transactions between independent parties. It provides quantitative evidence that your transfer prices are consistent with the arm's length principle.

Definition: Benchmarking is the process of identifying comparable independent transactions and using them as a reference point to establish arm's length prices for controlled transactions.

Key Components of a Benchmarking Study

1. Functional Analysis

The functional analysis examines the functions performed, assets employed, and risks assumed by each party to the transaction. This includes:

  • Activities and responsibilities of each party
  • Assets used in the transaction
  • Risks borne by each party
  • Contractual terms and conditions
  • Economic circumstances and industry factors

2. Economic Analysis

The economic analysis examines the economic factors that influence pricing, including:

  • Industry conditions and competitive dynamics
  • Market conditions and supply/demand factors
  • Product characteristics and differentiation
  • Geographic factors and market size
  • Regulatory environment

3. Comparable Company Analysis

This involves identifying and analyzing comparable independent companies that engage in similar transactions. The analysis includes:

  • Selection of comparable companies
  • Financial data collection and analysis
  • Calculation of relevant financial metrics
  • Determination of the interquartile range

Data Sources for Benchmarking

Primary Databases

  • Bureau van Dijk (BvD): Comprehensive financial data on companies worldwide
  • Thomson Reuters Eikon: Market data and financial information
  • S&P Capital IQ: Detailed company financial information
  • Amadeus: European company data
  • Orbis: Global company information

Secondary Sources

  • Public financial statements
  • Industry reports and analyses
  • Government statistics
  • Academic research

Transfer Pricing Methods and Benchmarking

Comparable Uncontrolled Price (CUP)

The CUP method directly compares prices charged in comparable uncontrolled transactions. Benchmarking involves finding comparable transactions between independent parties.

Resale Price Method

This method uses the resale price of products purchased from related parties as a starting point. Benchmarking identifies comparable resale margins in the industry.

Cost Plus Method

The cost plus method adds an appropriate markup to costs. Benchmarking identifies comparable markups in the industry for similar products and services.

Profit Split Method

This method allocates combined profit based on each party's contribution. Benchmarking helps determine appropriate profit allocation percentages.

Best Practices in Benchmarking

1. Use Multiple Data Sources

Don't rely on a single database or source. Use multiple sources to validate your findings and ensure robustness.

2. Ensure Comparability

Carefully assess the comparability of selected companies. Consider functional, contractual, and economic similarities and differences.

3. Document Your Methodology

Clearly document your benchmarking methodology, including data sources, selection criteria, and adjustments made.

4. Update Regularly

Benchmarking studies should be updated annually to reflect current market conditions and data.

5. Consider Multiple Scenarios

Analyze different scenarios and sensitivities to understand the range of acceptable transfer prices.

Pro Tip: Consider engaging specialized TP professionals to conduct your benchmarking studies. Their expertise and access to premium databases can significantly strengthen your compliance position.

Common Benchmarking Challenges

  • Finding truly comparable companies or transactions
  • Data quality and availability issues
  • Adjusting for differences between comparable and controlled transactions
  • Determining appropriate statistical ranges
  • Keeping benchmarking studies current

Conclusion

Benchmarking studies are essential for demonstrating the arm's length nature of your transfer prices. By following best practices and using robust methodologies, you can develop benchmarking studies that withstand tax authority scrutiny and provide strong support for your transfer pricing positions.
